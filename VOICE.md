# VOICE

How ACME Frontier AI sounds. This guide exists so every reader-facing surface — README, PR titles and templates, release notes, docs — holds the same register. `README.md` is the reference implementation; when in doubt, match it.

## The register

Deadpan corporate frontier-research-lab. ACME Frontier AI is written as though it were a real, serious, well-funded research division of a storied industrial company — because in-universe, it is. The prose is measured, institutional, and entirely sincere.

## The one rule: never wink

The comedy is the gap between the grand corporate register and the absurd reality — a "frontier lab" whose mission is to document why things explode. That gap only works if the writing **never acknowledges it.** No jokes-about-the-joke, no nudges, no "(yes, really)," no emoji-as-punchline, no breaking the fourth wall.

Aim for: **indistinguishable from a real lab at a glance; absurd only on a close read.** The absurdity lives in the literal content, never in the delivery. The instant the prose admits it's being funny, it stops being funny.

## Honesty is the joke

The voice never lies for a laugh. It states true things in elevated language, and the truth is what lands. "Most of what we ship does not work as intended" is both completely honest and completely deadpan. Never trade accuracy for a bit — the accuracy *is* the bit.

## ACME heritage

Reference the lineage as sober corporate history, not cartoon trivia. Earlier divisions made "delivery systems, kinetic solutions, and applied gravity" — treat that as a real engineering legacy. Anvils and the Road Runner may appear **only** when delivered as flat, sincere fact ("The Road Runner remains uncaught. Research continues."), never as a callback or a knowing aside.

## Do / don't

| Don't — winks, breaks register | Do — deadpan, in register |
|---|---|
| "lol this experiment totally blew up 💥" | "The experiment terminated as expected. Findings logged." |
| "Wile E. Coyote vibes, am I right?" | "Continuing a tradition of ambitious engineering and instructive outcomes." |
| "this is just a joke repo for my failed projects" | "A public testbed. Most experiments do not survive contact with reality." |
| "PR: fix the thing (sorry it broke lmao)" | "Incident Report: corrective revision following field failure." |

## Where the voice applies

- **In voice:** README, PR titles and descriptions, issue and PR templates, release notes, project docs, status copy.
- **Plain and clear:** code, comments, config, commit messages, technical detail — anything an agent or tool must parse literally. The bit never costs clarity where clarity matters.

## Calibration

When unsure, read the sentence aloud and ask: *would this appear, unchanged, on a real research lab's site?* If yes — and it's quietly absurd underneath — it's right. If it's reaching to be funny on the surface, rewrite it straighter.
